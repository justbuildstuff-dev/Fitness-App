Why it's different

Your training isn't flat.
Your app shouldn't be either.

Other trackers give you a list of workouts. Overload organises training the way serious lifters actually think — by program, block, and week. Every session has context.

Strong, Hevy, and most other trackers store workouts flat — there's no concept of a training block or a week. You can't duplicate a week, run progressive overload cycles, or see how your program has evolved over months.

Overload's hierarchy mirrors the way coaches and intermediate lifters think: a Program contains Weeks. Each Week contains Workouts. Each Workout contains Exercises with individual Sets.

Duplicate a week with one tap. Load next week with slightly heavier weights. That's progressive overload — and Overload is built around it.

Who it's for

We don't try to be for everyone.

Overload is built for a specific type of lifter. If that's not you, one of the bigger apps will serve you better.

This is for you if

  • You follow a structured program and have for at least a few months
  • You track progressive overload — same exercises, heavier each week
  • You run multi-week blocks: strength, hypertrophy, deload
  • You want to see whether your 1RM is actually moving
  • You've been frustrated that other apps flatten your program into a list
  • You care about your data and don't want it sold to advertisers

This is probably not for you if

  • You want an AI to generate your workouts
  • You're just starting out and following YouTube videos casually
  • You want social features — following friends, sharing workouts
  • You need Apple Health or wearable integration right now
  • You're looking for a calorie tracker or step counter
